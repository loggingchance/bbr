Federal clean-electricity credits

For combustion or gasification projects, lifecycle greenhouse-gas analysis can be central to clean-electricity credit eligibility and emissions-rate determination.

0.6¢per kWh, 2026 base applicable amount
3.1¢per kWh, 2026 increased applicable amount
Higher amounts generally depend on prevailing-wage and apprenticeship rules, subject to applicable exceptions. Potential bonuses may apply for domestic content and energy-community location.

6%base credit
30%with qualifying prevailing-wage & apprenticeship requirements

What a strong LCA does for the project

1

Tax & financing readiness

Creates a traceable emissions framework, surfaces evidence gaps early, and gives tax advisors, lenders and investors a defensible technical record.

2

Project strategy before filing

Tests feedstock, transport, facility and counterfactual assumptions before they become expensive commitments — and shows where better data could change the answer.

3

Operational insight

Reveals the stages that drive emissions and cost, supports scenario comparisons, and targets practical improvements in sourcing, logistics, energy use and production.

4

Reusable evidence base

Creates a structured record that can support later grants, customer questions, diligence, reporting, permitting discussions and future model updates.

For combustion and gasification facilities, lifecycle emissions can be part of the federal emissions-rate determination — not optional context.

FAQ

Questions we expect early in the conversation.

The right scope depends on the decision, the facility, the feedstock and the quality of available data.

Does my project need an LCA for §45Y or §48E?

For electricity-producing projects where eligibility depends on a lifecycle greenhouse-gas emissions rate, lifecycle analysis can be central to the credit determination. The exact requirement depends on the facility, feedstock and current Treasury/IRS rules, so we scope this with the project team and tax counsel.

How long does an LCA take, and what does it cost to scope?

It depends mostly on system complexity and data readiness. A short scoping review can usually identify the likely work, missing evidence and major methodological issues before anyone commits to a full analysis.

What data will we need to provide?

Typical inputs include feedstock quantities and sources, transport distances and loads, electricity and fuel use, production volumes, process details, facility records and supporting documents. We translate technical requirements into plain-language information requests for the people most likely to know the answers.

Do you work with our tax counsel and engineers?

Yes. LCA work for tax, financing or project diligence is strongest when the technical analysis, engineering facts and tax interpretation stay connected. We provide the lifecycle and evidence framework; tax counsel makes the final tax determination.

What standards or methods do you follow?

We start with the controlling regulatory or decision framework, then use established LCA methodology and applicable forest-sector literature. We document the methodology and sources used for each engagement rather than applying a generic template.

Can the LCA be reused for grants, customers or reporting?

Often, yes. A well-structured source-linked analysis can become a reusable evidence base for grants, customer questions, lender diligence, reporting and future model updates, although each later use may require its own scope or review.
